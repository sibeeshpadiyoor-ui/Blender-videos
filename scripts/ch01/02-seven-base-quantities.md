# Chapter 1, Video 2: Why only seven base quantities?

- **Series:** Fundamentals of Physics · **Section:** 1-1 Measuring Things
- **Length:** about 65 seconds, vertical (Reels / Shorts)
- **One idea:** we cannot give every quantity its own standard, so the world agreed on seven base quantities, and every other unit is built from them.
- **Voice:** same as Video 1 (Kokoro `am_michael`), a pause after every sentence.

## Notes for the reviewer (not narrated)
- The book (section 1-1): the 14th General Conference on Weights and Measures picked seven base quantities in 1971; 1 watt = 1 kg·m²/s³.
- The book's own table lists only length, time and mass. The other four (electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance, luminous intensity) come from the standard SI list; the units are ampere, kelvin, mole, candela.
- Challenge answer, revealed at the start of Video 3: speed, because it is length divided by time.
- The examples (watt, speed) are the book's own; the wording and visuals are original.
