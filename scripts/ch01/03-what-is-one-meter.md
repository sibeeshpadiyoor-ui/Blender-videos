# Chapter 1, Video 3: What is one meter, exactly?

- **Series:** Fundamentals of Physics · **Section:** 1-1 Measuring Things
- **Length:** about 75 seconds, vertical (Reels / Shorts)
- **One idea:** the meter has been redefined three times, each time to be more accurate and more available; today it is defined by the speed of light.
- **Voice:** same as the earlier videos (Kokoro `am_michael`), a pause after every sentence.

## Notes for the reviewer (not narrated)
- All dates and numbers are from the book (section 1-1, Length): the meter of 1792 (one ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the equator), the platinum-iridium bar near Paris, 1 650 763.73 wavelengths of orange-red krypton-86 light in 1960, and the 1983 definition (the distance light travels in a vacuum in 1/299 792 458 of a second, so that c is exactly 299 792 458 m/s).
- The book gives no date for the metal bar, so the script does not give one either.
- Challenge answer, revealed at the start of Video 4: 299 792 458 m/s × 10⁻⁹ s ≈ 0.3 m, about 30 cm.
- The long number is spoken in words (Kokoro can stumble on large numerals) and shown in digits on screen.
- Wording and visuals are original; the history and figures are facts from the book.
