# Chapter 1, Video 4: How do you change units without mistakes?

- **Series:** Fundamentals of Physics · **Section:** 1-1 Measuring Things
- **Length:** about 85 seconds, vertical (Reels / Shorts)
- **One idea:** to change units, multiply by a conversion factor that equals 1, and let the unwanted unit cancel.
- **Source:** Halliday & Resnick, section 1-1, "The International System of Units" and "Changing Units" (chain-link conversion). The 1792 metric system and the 10-hour day are from the book's Figure 1-1 caption. The examples and wording are original.
- **Opening:** the answer to Video 3's challenge first, then the new title (the same pattern as Video 3).

## Notes for the reviewer (not narrated)
- Challenge answer, revealed at the start of Video 5: 36 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 10 m/s.
- Video 3's challenge: 299 792 458 m/s × 10⁻⁹ s = 0.2998 m ≈ 30 cm.
- The book teaches this as **chain-link conversion**: multiply by conversion factors written as ratios equal to unity, and treat each number together with its unit. The rule "if the units do not cancel, invert the factor" is also from the book.
- The book's 1792 caption says the metric system proposed a 10-hour day. The video does not say who proposed it or any details beyond that.
- The challenge uses "kilo", which leads into Video 5 (prefixes).
