## Notes for the reviewer (not narrated)
- Challenge answer, revealed at the start of Video 6: 1 mm = 10⁻³ m and 1 μm = 10⁻⁶ m, so 1 mm = 1000 μm.
- Video 4's challenge answer used here: 36 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 10 m/s.
- From the book: scientific notation (powers of 10), the table of prefixes (kilo 10³, milli 10⁻³, micro 10⁻⁶, nano 10⁻⁹ are four of its most-used ones), and the idea that attaching a prefix to an SI unit multiplies it by that factor.
- Only the four most common prefixes are taught here, to keep one clear idea. The full table (giga, mega, centi and the rest) is in Table 1-2 of the book.
- The mass base unit is the kilogram, which already contains a prefix; this video avoids that case on purpose. It belongs in the mass video (Video 7).
